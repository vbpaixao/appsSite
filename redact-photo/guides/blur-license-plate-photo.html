How to Blur a License Plate in a Photo

Redact Photo Guides · Updated July 2026 · 3 min read

Quick answer: Open the photo in Redact Photo, draw a rectangle over the plate with manual selection (zoom in for precision), and apply blur, pixelation, or a black box. Export the masked copy for your listing or post.

Car listing photos, driveway shots, street photography, dashcam frames: license plates end up in public posts constantly, and a plate is more identifying than most people assume. Masking one takes about fifteen seconds when the tool is built for it.

Why Bother Blurring Plates

A visible plate ties a photo to a registration, a household, and a location: useful information for scammers running plate-cloning schemes, and for anyone you'd rather not have connecting your car to your address. Marketplaces are the classic case: your listing shows your car, your street, and your plate in one frame. Masking the plate costs nothing and removes the link.

Mask a Plate with Precision (Step by Step)

  1. Open the listing photo in Redact PhotoProcessing is on-device, your photos aren't uploaded to a “free plate blur” website's servers.
  2. Zoom in and select the plateUse manual region selection with zoom for a tight rectangle over the plate, pixel-perfect beats approximately-covered.
  3. Choose the maskPixelation or a black box reads cleanest on plates; strong blur works too. All three are flattened into the export.
  4. Export and repeat for each listing photoSave the masked copy and do the same for the other angles, front, back, three-quarter: before uploading the listing.

Which Mask Looks Right on a Listing

Buyers expect masked plates, so don't overthink aesthetics: pixelation looks professional, black box looks decisive, blur looks casual. What matters is coverage, zoom in after applying to confirm no digits peek out at the edges.

The Rest of the Photo Talks, Too

Listing photos taken at home can carry GPS coordinates in their metadata, and house numbers or street signs may be visible in frame. Text detection catches the signs; the EXIF panel strips the location, the complete routine is in sharing photos anonymously.
