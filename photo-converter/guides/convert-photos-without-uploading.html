How to Convert Photos Without Uploading Them to a Website

Photo Converter Guides · Updated July 2026 · 3 min read

Quick answer: Use an on-device converter instead of a web tool. Photo Converter runs 100% on your iPhone: select photos → Share → “Convert with PhotoConverter” → convert. No server ever sees your photos.

Search “HEIC to JPG” and the top results are websites: free, instant, no signup. The price is in the mechanics — a “free online converter” works by taking possession of your photos. For wallpapers, who cares. For photos of your kids, your ID, your home, your documents? Different question.

What Really Happens When You Use a Converter Site

Your photo is uploaded to a server you know nothing about, processed, stored for some period the privacy policy words vaguely, and offered back for download — usually alongside ad trackers. Even honest operators keep temporary copies; dishonest ones keep everything. You can't audit which kind you got, and EXIF data (including GPS coordinates) rides along with the upload.

On-Device Conversion: The Same Convenience, None of the Exposure

Modern iPhones convert images in milliseconds — there is no technical reason the job needs a server at all. An on-device converter does the identical work inside your phone's sandbox: nothing transmitted, nothing retained by a third party, works in airplane mode. That's Photo Converter's entire design: 100% on-device processing, verifiable by the fact it converts with Wi-Fi and cellular off.

Privacy Compounds

On-device conversion plus metadata stripping covers both halves of photo privacy: no third party sees the file, and the copy you share carries no hidden data. It matters most exactly where converter sites are most used — IDs and paperwork, family photos, anything you wouldn't hand to a stranger.
